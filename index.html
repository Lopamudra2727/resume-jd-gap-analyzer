How the score is calculated

The score is a weighted sum of six components. If a component doesn't apply (for example, the JD states no years of experience), it is dropped and the other weights are scaled up to fill the gap.

ComponentWeightWhat it measures
Required skills45%Weighted coverage of skills in the JD's required sections. Matched = 100% credit, inferred (e.g. Django ⇒ Python) = 75%, related tool (e.g. Flask for FastAPI) = 50%.
Preferred skills15%Same credit rules, for "nice to have", "bonus" and "familiarity with" items.
JD keyword coverage10%How many of the JD's 15 most frequent non-skill terms appear in your resume, weighted by frequency. Company names and boilerplate are filtered out, and domain words (latency, inventory, compliance…) rank higher.
Years of experience15%Years from your merged employment dates compared with the JD minimum. Below the minimum, the score is (yours ÷ required)1.2.
Education5%Highest degree vs. the JD's minimum. One level short = 60, or at least 75 if the JD accepts equivalent experience.
Resume quality (ATS)10%11 checks: contact details, standard section headings, quantified bullets (40%+), action verbs (60%+), weak phrasing, length, pronouns.

Skill importance in the JD: soft skills count ×0.5, each extra mention adds +15% (up to 4 extra), and skills in the job title count ×1.3. Learning-time estimates run from about 10 h (difficulty 1) to 200 h (difficulty 5) to reach working proficiency, halved if you already know a related tool.
